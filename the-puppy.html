The First Six Months – 1999

Was placed on a Northwest Airlines flight number 844 leaving from Minneapolis, Minnesota on the 20th of August at 7:10 A.M. My head was spinning!!! Before I left, I had all sorts of physical exams and shots. Arrived at Logan Airport in Boston around noon and my owner was waiting there for my arrival. We left for my new home in Hingham, MA.

Spent the first few weeks getting acclimated to my new master and his family. I sure did create a lot of excitement. Met Brett, John, Molly, Katy, Lauren and Kayla. Very nice people. I could tell that my master's wife wanted no part of me. Overheard her telling a friend on the phone when asked what I looked like, "he's black and he yips". Talk about giving a guy a chance {:<].

My training started early and it was done with food. My master kept saying "sitz" to me and pushing my butt down. Didn't like it at first but soon realized that every time I cooperated I received a piece of turkey hotdog. We did this in fifteen minute sessions about four times a day.

Next came the word "platz" (did I tell you he was teaching me in German). "Platz" means "down" in German. Same thing…lots of turkey frank bits. Got to be second nature to me.

Month of January 2000

Was taken down to see Mike Kelly at Canine Communications to have my ears taped. What he did was to take two rubber hair curlers, place some special glue on them, and then proceed to place them in my ears. It was not fun. Had to keep them in for three days in order to strengthen the cartilage at the base of my ears. I remember when I was real little, my ears were up like those of a bat. They went down when I started teething. They're looking about 90% now.

Month of February 2000

Well February has come to an end and I have to tell you, it hasn't been much fun. Kind of boring. Cold, snow and sleet are the order of the month. Went to see Mike Kelly for some more training. My master got a new "truck/SUV" and keeps telling me that I am taking over his life. Been learning "long stays" and handling myself in the back of the new vehicle.

My health has been pretty good except for some joint pain in my front legs. The vet said that it is common in GSDs and that it's from growing too fast. Gave me some medication which made a big difference. I was at the Norwell Animal Hospital and got on the scale. 77.8 pounds!!! How about that, folks.

Still commuting between the two houses on a daily basis but I hear that we are moving to a permanent location on March 30th. I am going to put some more pictures on the home page. Hope you enjoy them.

Month of March 2000

Went to the Norwell Animal Hospital to have them do some tests to determine just what this problem with my front legs is. I had some x-rays taken and sure enough, Dr. Morena confirmed his suspicion that I was suffering from Panosteitis.

Panosteitis is a disease primarily affecting young dogs of large breeds. It is characterized by inflammation within the bones, especially those of the legs. Lameness of one or more legs is often apparent as it has been in my case. I was told that the disease should persist for 1–6 months, with the average case lasting 2–3 months. During the course of the disease, periods of pain and lameness are interrupted by intervals of good health. Lameness may switch from one leg to the other and the degree of discomfort may vary. You could see it on the x-ray and I was told to continue to take Rimadyl as needed. By the way, weighed in at 84.5 lbs.

Months of April/May 2000

Well, my owner has finally moved to the new house and that means no more commuting. I have my own full blown kennel with two other cages/runs. I am psyched…that means I am going to have a play mate at some time. Hope one of them is a "fox" (if you get my drift).

I am still having some problems with Panosteitis which has now shown up in my left back leg. I was walking on three legs this morning. My owner got me right to the hospital and I had all kinds of tests. Had my hips X-rayed for the second time and the vet thought that the hips looked very good (for my age). That really doesn't mean too much until they look good when I am fifteen to eighteen months. Have some new medication called Etogesic. Has taken me off the Rimadyl. Rest for two weeks. Also got some tick and flea treatment called "Frontline" and some Otomax for my ears. I am not real fond of having my ears cleaned and as a result had a bit of a yeast infection. This stuff should take care of it.

While I was at the hospital my owner had a microchip inserted in the back of my neck which will enable veterinarians or dog pounds to scan my back and know the name and phone number of my owner. Like a canine "Lojack" {:<]. I get the sense that this guy really cares about me.

That's it for now… come back and visit from time to time.

Month of June, 2000

Well, there is certainly a great deal of activity around here. A group of men have been working for the past six hours putting up a humungous tent and I am told that I am going to have my first "overnight" at Mike Kelly's K9 Communications in Norwell. I am psyched!

There was a carpenter working outside my kennel door. People keep calling him the "S Man". He sure made a racket. I was pleasantly surprised, however, to find that he had built me a small deck where I was going to be able to lay and just relax. I overheard someone saying that three runs are going up on Tuesday.

Sure enough, on Tuesday my runs were taking shape. Just one more piece across the back and it will be done.

Months June/July/August 2000

I have received a large number of e-mails complaining about the lack of updating on this page. Guilty, guilty, guilty. I have just been so busy. Let me bring you up to date. My Panosteitis seems to have calmed down. My master had me on medication for about a month and a couple of weeks began to wean me off of it. At present I am medication free and feeling pretty well.

I have been going to Mike Kelly's K9 Communications school and was tested/evaluated to see whether I am at the stage where I can start my protection training. Did pretty well. The test was pretty interesting. Was in Mike's training room when there was a knock on the door. My owner whispered a word in German which means "Guard". As soon as he said it, this person in a Halloween mask rushed into the room and scared the hell out of me. I started barking and got very hyper. He left and things were back to normal. A minute later, the same thing happened. After about four tries, I began to realize that the word my master uttered meant for me to get "turned on" and bark. Hey, I like this {:<]. I guess I passed my test with flying colors, because my master told me that we start the official protection training next Friday. It goes on for a very long time and I am told is fun. I am psyched!!!

Months September/October/November

Well, I have been very busy with my protection training. It mostly takes place on Friday nights at Mike Kelly's K9 Communications in Norwell. There are about 10 to 15 Shepherds there every week and we each take our turn and get several chances to bite the sleeve (which I really like to do). I feel really confident and get lots of praise from my master and Mike. I am going to try and get some pictures of my training the next time I go to Mike's. Happy Thanksgiving everyone.

Months December/January/February

It is cold, wet and sometimes snowy. Things have come to somewhat of a grinding halt. The protection training stops due to cold weather and snow. Not very safe this time of year. Spend a lot of time with my master in his office and take a couple of long walks each day for exercise. It won't be long now before the weather will begin to improve and I will be able to get back to Mike's. Really miss it!

Months March through August 2001

Did I say it was cold in the paragraph above? Well wouldn't you know, it was 101 yesterday. Water, water everywhere and I drank every drop I could slap the old tongue on. My master is very good about that. Keeps that water bucket full for the kid.

Lots of exciting news. Still training at Mike Kelly's. Finally got the big guy to bring a camera with us when we go to Mike's. After all, I am running my tail off biting sleeves and having a jolly old time and I have nothing to show for it. Now I do! Are you ready for this? Here I am, waiting my turn to bite the sleeve. Here's another one of me drooling to get the old choppers on that sleeve. Ahhh, at last.
