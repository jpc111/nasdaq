Nasdaq's Training Record

Plymouth County Sheriff's Department K9 Academy
November 12, 2003 through March 25, 2004

PCSD Diary

November 12, 2003 (Day 1)

Arrived at the academy at 0800 hours. This was the first day of classes. Spent a portion of the day with Lt. Correia explaining the curriculum and laying out the ground rules as to timing and safety.

Went tracking later in the morning. Lt. Corriea laid the tracks, let them age and then we walked behind the team that was being evaluated. Lt Correia gave me a demonstration on how, in the future, we would be sending Nasdaq off on a track. He explained their use of "scent pads" and how he wanted me to approach them. He then created three of them and had Nasdaq and I approach each of the pads and begin a track. We then broke for lunch and went back to the office and watched portions of an Ed Frawley Video on tracking.

In the afternoon, we went to an old house on the prison property and two dogs gave a demonstration of a building search. They then did some agitation work with the same two dogs. The session ended at 4:00 PM.

November 13, 2003 (Day 2) Weather Windy (50 to 60 MPH gusts) 37 Degrees. Very cold wind chill

We met at the Marshfield Fairground at 8:00 AM as planned and prepared for a busy day. The first thing we did was to get tested in an agility exercise for the dog. We were brought into an old horse stable/barn where students were to have their dogs climb a stairs (the incline of which was extremely steep) and then come back down. We were the third team to attempt the climb and Nasdaq handled it without any difficulty. Upon reaching the top he just lay there and waited for my next command. Upon receiving it he carefully descended the stairs/ladder without incident. He performed the task another time with equal success.

It has become obvious to me that Nasdaq is not very happy being in a crate for a good portion of the day. He is used to full access to the back of my car but for safety reasons (which I agree with) Lt. Correia wanted Nasdaq in a crate at all times while in the car.

We then went tracking and Nasdaq was introduced to a very simple track. A problem arose when I tried to place the lead under his front leg. The dog bit me. There is no simple way of explaining this. I was shocked. It was not a hard bite and my skin was not broken. In all the tracking and protection training Nasdaq has worn a harness. Suspect that he is not too happy with the leash on his collar and being strung under his front leg. Will have to keep a close eye on the development. Lt. Correia, although he is not a fan of harnesses, allowed me to use the harness for the track

I was then given a building sweeping demonstration using a canine. Nasdaq and I did not participate in this exercise but Lt. Correia wanted me to get an idea of what was to come and how it was done. The other teams performed extremely well. Very impressive! I am told that I am going to have to "volunteer" for this bite suit duty. Oh joy :<).

The Lt. then proceeded to lay some tracks for the various teams to follow. I followed behind without Nasdaq and Lt. Correia explained to me the importance of watching how the dogs act in different situations along the tracks. These were not simple straight line tracks, but tracks laid for dogs who had been at the academy for many more weeks or for teams that were doing "in service" training.

The sessions ended at 4:00 PM.

November 14, 2003 (Day 3) Weather windy (30 to 40 MPH) 34 Degrees Bitter wind chill Dry

Outside weather conditions were not good so we went to the academy gymnasium where we spent the morning doing agility testing on a course that was set up for this purpose. The course consisted of five 4' high fences, a window fence, some stacked tires, a black tunnel and a 5' high obstacle created from folded gymnasium mats. There were four canine teams present. Nasdaq performed all tasks well. He did need a little coaxing through the black tunnel but did go through several times during the test. All of the dogs seemed to have some difficulty with this obstacle. It was a bit unusual but an excellent training aid.

Each team then began to demonstrate obedience while going through a series of commands (heal, sit, stay, come, left and right turns at heal). Nasdaq performed these tasks well and without difficulty.

During lunch we continued to watch the 3.5-hour Leerburg video tape on RCMP Tracking and also saw a tape of a prisoner extraction during a disturbance that took place several years ago at the prison.

The weather did not get much better but the training had to continue so we all went to one of the deserted farm houses on the property and practiced building sweeps. This exercise was performed with a person hiding in a large protective box. The canine team would approach to front door, give three loud warnings that the canine was present and was going to be sent if the person did not give up. The warning was given three times, and hearing no surrender language from the hiding person, the dog was sent into the house to locate and bite the target. Each team performed this exercise. A modified adaptation of the sweep was designed for Nasdaq in order to get him used to the noise, sounds of the warnings and the door opening. Lt. Correia conducted minor agitation, which got Nasdaq turned on and ready to go. Nasdaq entered the house and bit a sleeve. Lt. Correia noted that his bite was not as hard as it should have been and we agreed that it was due to too much "out" training ….the dog was waiting for an "out" (stop biting) command as is usually the case in his previous protection training. He thought Nasdaq needed a little more agitation work without any "out" commands for a while.

Lt. Correia then took Nasdaq and I to a new area of the property and set up a tether to a tree so he could get Nasdaq some drive building experience in order to solve the soft bite issue. He was given about 4 to 5 minutes of agitation and demonstrated an improvement in the force of his bite. This buildup of drive will be done on a daily basis to bring out the proper level of aggression needed in K9 law enforcement. Nasdaq responded well, and as usual, really enjoyed the bite work.

The training ended at 4:00 PM.

November 15, 2003 (Day 4) - Weather Clear 31 Degrees

Today (or rather tonight) was our first Plymouth County Canine Academy exposure to "night training". As instructed, we met at the academy office sharply at 7:00 PM. There was another student team and an "in service" deputy present for the exercises.

Nasdaq was exposed to a new situation this evening…….A MUZZLE. The muzzle is used in many training and real-world situations and it is important that Nasdaq be able to wear one. Well, I have to tell you, he did not like this new experience. He was jumping all around trying to pull the muzzle off but after a while was able to tolerate it. He had it on for a good part of the evening. There will be more of this to come as the training continues.

While Nasdaq was wrestling with his muzzle the two other teams practiced "area sweeps" where a person is sent to hide in the woods and a canine (muzzled) is sent to locate and subdue the person. This was practiced for a couple of hours and I stayed with Lt. Correia who explained the tactical implications of the exercise and it's use.

Lt. Correia then laid tracks for all three teams (including Nasdaq) and we spent considerable time tracking around office buildings, construction sites and back parking lots.

Following the tracking we all did some agitation work simulating a "crowd control" scenario. We pulled a police car into the middle of a parking lot with all lights flashing. Three teams lined up a safe distance apart and Lt. Correia hid behind a dumpster and jumped out cracking a whip and yelling. The dogs, who were muzzled, responded appropriately and proceeded to keep the Lt. at bay and forced him to retreat to behind the dumpster. At this point the canines were "outed" and "platzed" to await the next "assault". They came, one after another ….three or four. The dogs had a blast!

By this time it had to be 2:00 am and it was cold. We headed back to the office and watched a videotape of canines being trained and a tape on a DPO certification.

We then went to a deserted house on the Prison property and did some "house sweeps". Guess who wore the bite suit? I hid in a room during an exercise where there were two people hiding in the house. It was pitch black. As the dogs searched you could hear them sucking in the scent and the pitter-patter of toe nails. All of a sudden WHAM!! Both dogs (on two separate occasions) bit me on the back and not the arm. Won't say it was fun but it definitely was an experience. Suspect there will be more to come. Have to do my share, right? NOT :<).

We called it quits at 3:00 AM ….sack time. Great night!! Tomorrow night we go to Raynham.

November 16, 2003 (Day 5) Weather partly to mostly cloudy. Low 36F. Winds light and variable.

Showed up at Raynham, MA at 7:00 PM. Lt. Correia showed up a few minutes later and said that there was an armed robbery in Fall River and that he had been called out to assist. Training session was cancelled and will be scheduled at a later date.

November 19, 2003 (Day 5 Continued)

The training day started at 8:00 AM at a junkyard in Carver. Several dogs did some drug finds and then we did some agitation and sleeve work. Lt. Correia hid behind a car and when Nasdaq saw him I announced myself and turned the dog on. He responded well. He was given a bite. I thought he was a little chattery, but Lt. Correia said nothing. These exercises took about three hours and we headed back to the office for lunch.

After lunch we went down to the gun range on the prison property where we all went through a gun fire drill. The drill consisted of walking the dog at heal. Giving him a "Platz" and then firing a gun. We then calmed the dog and walked about 20 more paces and did the same thing. This was done three other times. On each occasion, Nasdaq went to a sit position however he kept his ground each time. He did a good job for the first time out. It was a big improvement from when I took him to my gun club for the first time.

We then went to the front gate of the old prison and Lt. Correia laid a 250-yard track for Nasdaq right along the edge of Orby Street. He placed a piece of hotdog about every 15 steps and put one left hand turn in the track. Nasdaq did well not being distracted by the cars whizzing by. The track was completed well and he was rewarded with a bag of hotdogs. Nasdaq's tracks are increased in length a little bit every day. He seems to be doing well. I am sure they will get more difficult as each day passes. We are taking one day at a time and loving it!

November 20, 2003 (Day 6)

The day started at 8:00 AM at the office where Lt. Correia announced that the Plymouth County and Bristol County K9 units were going to team up with the Plymouth Police Department and do a drug sweep of Plymouth North High School. When the Bristol K9 folks arrived we proceeded to the Plymouth Police Department for a briefing by a Plymouth PD Captain. It was decided that there would be four dog teams on the lockers and in some of the classrooms and two dog teams would cover the cars in the parking lots.

I shadowed one of the Plymouth K9 teams and found the experience to be extremely interesting. We did two classrooms and a girl's locker room. Came up with nothing. We then went to the parking lot and helped out there. Again, no hits.

All the teams together wound up with two arrests for misdemeanor possession. The school administration worked very well with us and were really into seeing that drugs are not in the schools.

In the afternoon Lt. Correia, and a Norfolk County Deputy went to the farm and did some agitation and bite work. The day ended at 4:00 PM

November 21, 2003 (Day 7)

It was pretty cold all day in the Plymouth area.

AT 8:00 AM we went to the gym and did some agility work with the dogs. Jumped fences, climbed tires, went through tunnels. Nasdaq did OK (not great). There were several other teams there.

After lunch we went to Camelot where Vinnie dressed in the suit and hid in the woods. We followed him, spotted him, gave three warnings and sent the dog in after him. He was bitten and then the dogs were pulled off the suit. Nasdaq did fairly well. He found Vinnie, bit him and took him to the ground. The other teams did a couple of tracks and we called it a day at 4:00 PM

November 22, 2003 (Day 8) Weather – 40 Degrees, clear skies

Tonight was an activity packed evening that started promptly at 7:00 PM. We found ourselves in Raynham at an abandoned Kmart store. We had four teams in training. The first thing Nasdaq and I did was to enter a portion of the shopping center, which had been torn apart and prepared for demolition. A deputy began some agitation and then fled into the main part of the building and hid behind a pile of materials. I was ordered to attempt a building search and gave four warnings to the "perp". Hearing no answer I sent the dog on his search with a bite command. The area to be searched was extremely large. The dog began to search the building by going into a "Rest Room" and finding nothing. He circled back to where I was standing at the entrance and was again sent. He again searched and within a minute came across the hiding Deputy. Nasdaq, who was muzzled, attacked the deputy who shouted out "I give up. Call off your dog". Lt. Correia and I rushed in and pulled Nasdaq off of the subject, had him raise his hands to be searched and then had Nasdaq watch (glare) as the subject ran off again. There was not a second apprehension. This method of not having a second apprehension was designed to keep the dog "on his toes" and alert in case there was a second person hiding in the area. This was Nasdaq's first building search. Lt Correia seemed to be pleased with Nasdaq's effort. I on the other hand thought that his intensity was somewhat less than the other canines. He assured me that he would improve with practice.

The next exercise involved a move to another store that was not as messy. All four teams entered the store and lined up at a safe distance from one another. Lt Correia then jumped from behind a wall in a menacing fashion and proceeded to agitate each dog up and down the line. We have previously made our weapons safe and while this agitation was going on we drew our guns while controlling our dogs. When Lt. Correia ran away the dogs were put in a "down/Platz" position, our guns were holstered. We awaited several more agitation attempts. Nasdaq performed very well, showed good aggression and control. He was really turned on.

After giving the dogs a breather we began to do our tracking. Lt. Correia laid a track for each team. This exercise takes several hours. Let me tell you a little about Nasdaq's track.

Lt Correia has a bad habit of sneaking away to lay these tracks and you can see that he uses every sinister trick he know (depending on the training level of the dog). The Lt. is really good at it.

When he came back he asked two questions:

  • How does Nasdaq do around cars? To which I answered that I did not think he would have a problem, but that I was not sure.
  • Is he likely to jump into water? On this question I said that I had absolutely no idea. At the same time I started to get that "what the hell does this guy have planned for me" feeling.

With these mindsets implanted in my head we went to the scent pad that had been established and began to track. Nasdaq was right on it and moving at a brisk pace. We passed by a drainage ditch with water in it and I asked if this was the water he was referring to. The answer was no. We tracked through brush, briars, and low hanging branches and up ahead I could see that the actual trail we were following was about to disappear. Lt. Correia then told me to be careful for I was coming upon a edge of a 15 to 20 foot cliff. There was no place to go and Nasdaq started to go over the edge. Somehow he found a path and began to follow it down to the back of what I was later to find out, the Taunton River, Ahhhh, the beautiful Taunton River.

At the bottom of the cliff which I indelicately slid down, we came to a highway overpass (we were under it) and Nasdaq took me upon some huge boulders that had been used to shore up the riverbank and the highway. We proceeded along the riverbank and then began to head upward away from the river. If there was ever a truer saying in tracking it is "trust your dog". At this point I had no choice. I had no idea where I was, three guys following behind me not inclined to offer much assistance and my butt was dragging. I fell climbing a hill, but got right up. I began to realize why a number of people had asked me over the past couple of years "why do you want to do this, are you nuts?"

We made it back to the top of the hill, which we had climbed down twenty minutes before. Nasdaq is taking me through some relatively easy ground and just as I was going to give him a "platz" so I could catch my breath he dropped on his own and stuck his muzzle into the leaves. What did he find, you ask? It was a bag of sliced hotdogs that had been buried there by Lt. Correia and which signified the end of the track. GOOD GOING NASDAQ!!!!!!

Several other tracks were done by the other teams and then we went back to one of the shopping center buildings. We all did some obedience work and when the dogs calmed down, Lt. Correia walked around with a sleeve on to which the dogs were not to respond. There lack of response was rewarded with a bite.

Nasdaq was then brought in and I did some obedience with him for 3 to 4 minutes. Lt. Correia then took me through a very interesting exercise. He pointed me toward a door about 40 yards away (I told you this was a big building) and told me to walk the dog to that door and then "Platz" him. I did this. We then entered a hallway and he had me do the same thing at a set of stairs. We climbed the stairs with four deputies behind me and came into a second story area with plywood floors, which made an unusual noise as we walked upon it. I "platzed" the dog and was then told to cross the room with the dog at "heal/Foos". As I did this all four men followed behind Nasdaq and I with the floor making this very harrowing noise. We did this a couple of more time. Lt. Correia told me later that this was an exercise designed to get Nasdaq used to having several backup officers walking behind us as we pursue a subject(s). Nasdaq was not phased and did what he was told. Lt. Correia's comment was "fine" which I have come to realize is high praise for the dog. The evening ended at 3:00 AM

November 23, 2003 ( Day 9) - Weather Clear and cold. 41 Degrees (it felt much colder)

Tonight was a big night. There were only three teams training which enabled us to accomplish a great deal.

Nasdaq worked on two major things this evening, tracking and intensity:

  • Intensity: One of the first exercises we did was a repeat of the previous nights agitation where Nasdaq was brought into the building, which was quite dilapidated. Nasdaq was wearing a muzzle. He and I took cover behind a huge roof air conditioner (which was on the floor and further testament to the condition of the building). A deputy (decoy) had been placed in the building and hidden from view. I proceeded to give warning to the decoy "Police canine, come out with your hands up. Let me see your hand!! I am going to send my dog. He will bite you". After the third repeat of this warning Nasdaq was given the "bite" command in German and was sent into the building to clear it. He wandered about the floor, which was at least 50 yards in length and came to an old restroom where the decoy had hidden. Suddenly there was a shriek from the decoy asking me to get the dog off him. I rushed to the dog (with Lt. Correia right behind me) and pulled the dog off of the decoy. To further increase his intensity, the decoy was permitted to run away and the warning was again given. Pursuit did not follow after Nasdaq showed us that he was really turned on (the way he is supposed to be). Nasdaq performed this exercise well but still has a way to go. I heard Lt. Correia's "fine" which is my sign that the dog performed well.

The next exercise, performed several hours after the above described one was again in a building that was deserted but in reasonable condition. Lt. Correia found a long hallway with four or five offices on each side of the corridor. After we discussed the scenario, Nasdaq was brought from the car and the two of us stationed ourselves at the beginning of the corridor. I again gave the customary warning but only got a chance to say it once when Lt. Correia jumped out of the first office and began to scream and crack a whip. He moved back and forth in front of Nasdaq (who had a double leash attached to my left hand) and proceeded to the next office door, which he closed. Nasdaq was given the bite command and attacked the door, trying to break it down. I then pulled him back and Lt. Correia again emerged and continued the agitation. Again he slipped into the next office and Nasdaq went to the door, trying to push his way through it. Well, this went on for five more doors. By the time we got to the last door, Nasdaq was really going at it. I called him off and took him to the car. There was no reward bite. He was left in a high state of agitation. About twenty minutes later we did the exercise again but this time Nasdaq was allowed into the last office where he had an opportunity to bite Lt. Correia, which after all was the object of the game. Job well done and the "fine" word from Lt. Correia.

  • Tracking: After last nights track I was not sure what to expect this evening, but we were ready for whatever Lt. Correia's devious mind could cook up. He took us out behind the building to a service parking lot where deliveries used to be made to the various stores within the mall. He indicated to me that the scent pad has been placed next to the right front tire of a parked car in the lot.

As usual, I downed/Platzed the dog and stroked his head and back to relax him and then gave him the "Suk/track" command. Nasdaq started off on a track and about 15 yards from the beginning Lt. Correia said, "bring him back". That is the opposite of "Fine" :<). (After the track was completed he suggested that I shorten the lead and make Nasdaq prove that he was on the correct track). On the second try Nasdaq and I heard nothing, which meant we were on the right track. Nasdaq followed a grass strip along the back of the lot (all the time I was expecting to take a sharp right turn into a jungle). There was no right turn into the jungle but instead we followed along the edge of the lot line, took a left hand turn which Nasdaq maneuvered perfectly and proceeded about three hundred yards in pretty much a straight line. We took another left turn by a highway (Nasdaq was not distracted by the passing cars). Following this leg of the track at about the 100-yard mark, Lt. Correia changed directions and got us a little off balance. I stopped the track, praised the dog (at this point Lt. Correia suggested that when I see a change of direction like this that I test it by planting my feet and making the dog "prove" that the track does, in fact, change direction …can't wait to try this technique next time out.

We continued the track and came upon Nasdaq's reward, a bag full of hotdog slices.

Because this track was a little easier on the handler it gave me an opportunity to learn some of the nuances from Lt. Correia on a real-time basis instead of worrying about falling off a cliff, which necessitates critiques being given at the end of the track

Good job, Nasdaq!

The evening ended at 2:35 AM. Sack time!

November 24, 2003 (day 10) - Weather Clear – 48 Degrees

The day started at 8:00 AM and it turned out to be a pretty trying day. It was one I do not want to repeat. We went down to Camelot and Lt. Correia laid about four tracks throughout the property. He chose Nasdaq to go first and we got ready to do so. The track started out well. Nasdaq picked up the scent pad and started off. I suspect the entire track was about ¼ of a mile and was about 35 minutes old. We followed it along with about four deputies in trail behind us. At about the ¾ mark, Nasdaq just quit. He just lost interest in the track. I coaxed him to no avail. He just wanted to go back to where he already had been. I rested him for a minute and we tried again. He finally picked it up again and made it to the end where he received his reward. Lt. Correia, when I asked what he thought the problem was, said that the guys following behind notice that there was a noise that sounded like gunfire a mile or so ahead of us in the track and they all saw Nasdaq shut down because of the gunfire. This was not good! I knew that had been a problem before I ever entered the academy and had informed Lt. Correia as such.

As we walked back to our starting point I asked Lt. Correia to "talk to me". His comment was that he did not want to waste my time if the dog was afraid of gunfire. We had to find out for sure.

We waited several hours while the other teams went through their tracking sessions, had a fast lunch and then went out to one of the fields on the prison property. As we left, I saw Lt. Correia carrying a shotgun and carrying two shotgun shells. He also had a revolver with him. The other guys tried to cheer me up saying all dogs have problems that have to be solved and that was what the academy was for. Lt. Correia's words to me on the first day "I am here to solve problems in the dogs should they arise". I don't mind telling you, when I saw that shotgun, I was worried. I figured it was going to be a long ride home. After all that work, all the years of training.

When we arrived at the field, Lt. Correia put on the bite suit and walked about 50 yards into the middle of the field. The test was that I was to bring the dog to a fence entrance to the field, call out my three warnings and then send the dog to bite.

I did as instructed, "Police canine, show me your hands or I am going to send my dog and he will bite you" As I did, Nasdaq got revved up and was ready to go. Suddenly Lt. Correia took the shotgun and fired it in the air. There was a tremendous blast and a foot of flames coming out of the barrel. Nasdaq become a little unsettled and turned into me. I then gave my second warning and Nasdaq responded. Again, a second blast. I gave Lt. Correia a moment to throw the shotgun away and put on a sleeve. As he was doing this I gave Nasdaq the bite command and set him loose. He charged at Lt. Correia and after Nasdaq had covered half the distance to him, the Lt. pulled out the handgun and fired three shots, one after another as the dog came upon him. Nasdaq did not flinch; he went for the Lt., bit him and held on. The two other teams who were way behind us were cheering him on. I slowly walked the fifty yards to the Lt. to allow Nasdaq to enjoy his bite.

Nasdaq passed his test; he will live another day. Good going partner!

The day ended at 4:00 PM

November 28 (Day 11) Weather Rain Temperature 53 (Nice Day for training)

Four teams met at The Paul A. Dever School in Taunton, which has a number of vacant buildings which are ideal for building searches. It is a sprawling facility.

We started outside with Lt. Correia laying four tracks, one for each team. When it came time for Nasdaq to go I was told that it would be a short track but that "objects" had been introduced on the track. In the real world, this could be a firearm, a wallet, a shell casing or other contraband. The track was straight ahead. The idea was that when the dog came to the item he was to lie down next to it and wait for his handler to pick it up. The first couple of items were large and I was able to see them. When Nasdaq came upon them I told him to down/platz. Of course, he did so. I picked up the item and handed it to the Lt. who was walking behind us. This continued for about seven (7) more items and by the time we got to the end Nasdaq was laying down without the command from me. This type of track will be practiced often. Evidence recovery is a very important function of a canine team.

When everyone had finished their tracks we began doing building searches. I was the decoy for three of the teams and had to hide in a drainpipe hole in the floor with a heavy metal cover placed over me. We had to wait four or five minutes so my scent would build up. Talk about feeling buried alive. This was not a fun thing to be doing but I had a flashlight with me and whenever I started feeling claustrophobic I turn it on to settle myself down. There was the usual warning, the pitter patter of dog feet, the deep breath that the dog takes just before he finds his target and then scraping and barking. Upon hearing the latter the handler comes up and leashes the dog while the cover is taken off the hole so I can get out. Being the decoy I put on an act for the dog. "I give up! Keep the dog off of me. Don't let him bite me". This builds up the confidence of the dog.

When it was Nasdaq's turn his exercise was a little different. A decoy hid behind a door and Nasdaq was sent to find him. He did and made the appropriate barking and clawing gestures.

Before we knew it, it was 1:45 PM so we took a fast lunch and came back to the building. Nasdaq, with a muzzle on, was sent into the building again to search out a decoy. This time the person (Scott Paul from the Fall River Police Department) was lying on top of some wooden lockers. He was just lying there perfectly quiet.

The idea of this exercise was to get the dog to be aware of the whole space, both high and low. Nasdaq was sent, entered and left the room twice, went into a couple of other rooms and then re-entered the room with the locker. He finally saw the decoy on top of the locker and "indicated" by jumping up to try and reach him and barking. I pulled him off, Scott made the usual "I give up" theatrics and I took Nasdaq out of the building.

After Nasdaq's turn I was then placed in my favorite role ….a decoy :<). This time Lt. Correia placed me in a wooden locker and turned the door toward the wall. I literally could not move. It was pitch black and I came close to the claustrophobic edge. Not a nice feeling. The dogs did their thing (none too soon as far as I was concerned) and we called it a day. Overall, a very good day.

November 29, 2003 (Day 12) Weather - clear and cold. Windy

The evening started an hour early at 6:00 PM. We met and trained at the Raynham Shopping Center. There were four teams (two from the Norfolk County Sheriff's Office, Scott Paul from Fall River and me).

We started doing a four-team lineup where Lt. Correia came out of the darkness and agitated four dogs at once (Nasdaq had a muzzle). We had cleared our weapons before we started the exercise and when the Lt. gave up after a few minutes we drew our weapons while holding the agitated dogs. We did this several times.

Then we each did a building search where each dog was sent to an area of this huge complex to find a decoy hiding somewhere. Nasdaq performed this operation well. He seems to improve each time he does it. He still has a way to go but I think he is getting the hang of it.

We then went outside (and it was cold) and began out tracking exercise. Nasdaq was given a fairly long track through some pretty lousy terrain. As we started it was noticed that Nasdaq had some bleeding from his mouth. I suspect he has bitten his tongue. This did not seem to bother him. We had to cross a small creek, into which Nasdaq tracked. He could have gone over a stone curbstone that was lying across the creek but I think the wind moved the scent and he thought the Lt. had walked across the creek. The water was nasty! It was cold and windy.

I pulled Nasdaq out of the water and without missing a step he started tracking again. He led me straight to his bag of hotdogs. He did a very fine job.

After several hours of this, we went back inside and tied a line to a lalley column and agitated two of the dogs further. Nasdaq was one of them.

November 30 (Day 13) Weather Cold – Sky clear

In this situation a very long line (50 feet or so) was attached to the dog and the handler, using a bulletproof shield, slowly approached the subject and released the dog for a bite. When the dog made contact and reacted to the pain, the line was used to pull the target off the chair that he was sitting in and closer so that he can safely be apprehended. Nasdaq performed this function well. Lt. Correia then did some other bite work and "run-a-ways" which enabled Nasdaq to have a little fun and at the same time improve his drive. He loved it.
  • Crowd Control – We then did some more work with the previous mentioned body shield. We had to hold the shield in our right hand, the dog at heal on our left side and do the "duck walk" both up and down the full length of the gym with the Lt. directly behind my right shoulder with his weapon drawn and providing cover for me. As usual, Nasdaq did well. I on the other hand can feel it today in the back of my legs. Not a very easy task.
  • Ladder Walk – A couple of weeks ago Nasdaq was given his first taste of walking on a house ladder up a slight incline. When he did it at that time he had some problems, as do most dogs on their first try. Well tonight we tried it again and there was a 500% improvement. He walked up the ladder with minimal coaxing and some better handling on my part. The Lt. says that the dog is going to have an easier time once the ladder is placed at a steeper (more normal) slant. That time will be coming. Anyway, Nasdaq performed this exercise four times and we ended the exercise with lots of well deserved praise.

Because of the dangerously cold weather there was no tracking. The evening ended at 3:00 AM

January 28, 2004 (Day 40) Weather snow & cold – +23 degrees

Officer Scott Paul and I participated in the training today.

We went to Camelot where Nasdaq did a track laid by Lt. Correia. The first half of the track was laid on a road and crossed the road about every 20 feet which gave Nasdaq plenty of practice with sharp left and right-hand turns. There were also two diagonal crossings. He performed these tasks flawlessly.

The track then entered the woods and followed back to the main road and then along that road where Nasdaq found his reward.

Next on the agenda was an on-lead area search where we simulated trying to find a person who might be lost in the woods (dead or alive). We approached the area so that there would not be any footprints of the lost person apparent and so the dog would have to try and scent. We walked in grids and covered the entire area. Lesson learned: the cone scent might only be about 15' in size so we would have to get pretty close to the target before finding it. Secondly, we should try to use the wind to our advantage.

The last exercise for the day was evidence recovery. I threw two objects that had been in my pockets and Nasdaq was sent to find them. He did this exercise very well and was awarded with hotdogs after each find. The Lt. then threw something from his pocket which was completely out of sight. It took a little longer for the dog to find the object, but he did.

The day ended at 4:00 PM

January 29, 2004 (Day 41) Weather Cold – +23 degrees

We had a new training participant today in the person of Officer Tim Wigmore of the Duxbury Police Department. Of course, fellow student Scott Paul from Fall River was also present.

Officer Wigmore had not been exposed to the "potato barn" so the first thing we did was drive over there to give his dog the experience. I donned the "pajama suit" and laid down in the "coffin" as I have done many times in the past. We waited for about 15 minutes for the scent to build up then Tim sent the dog (with muzzle) to find me. Took about twelve minutes, but seemed like four hours. God, I hate that place :<).

Since we were facing extremely low wind chill factors we went to the dairy farm for some building search. Each of us took turns climbing a step ladder and hiding in the rafters above the ceiling. We had our feet hanging down. Each dog was sent to find the "bad guy". The purpose of this exercise was to get the dog to search "high". All three dogs, including Nasdaq, performed this task extremely well.

In the afternoon we went to the gym and did some more obedience and bite work. We had the dogs jump over obstacles such as hurdles, windows and stacked tires. Nasdaq performed these tasks very well. He works off leash very well. It takes a few moments for the dogs to figure out why they have been brought in from the car. Are they looking for a bad guy, are they supposed to bite the first person they see or are they going to do agility and obedience. Once they figure that out they do just fine.

We also practiced having the dog walk the relatively horizontal ladder. Nasdaq had done this for the first time several days ago. He performed this difficult task three times. He gets better each time he does it.

Lt. Correia then had us do some bite work with the dogs jumping through one of the previously mentioned windows and biting a sleeve. Nasdaq did this three times and did it well.

The day ended at 4:00 PM.

January 30, 2004 (Day 42) Weather 23 degrees – windy

Today was a big tracking day. Officer Tim Wigmore and Officer Scott Paul were in attendance.

The first track was laid for Nasdaq behind the bus company and went for about one mile. The track was about one hour old. It was very cold and there was snow on the ground. Once we made it up a hill and through some woods we came to a back road and followed it for a considerable distance. Nasdaq followed the track with enthusiasm and was right on it until we came to an intersection. Nasdaq seemed to lose the scent and we had to perform a couple of "bring him back Paul". It appears that I missed a couple of head movements on Nasdaq's behalf, which really messed things up. I got very frustrated and couldn't figure out what the problem was. I am told that the dog looked left and then right (which meant that he had come to a corner and that I should have planted my feet) and instead I just kept going until the dog had obviously lost the track. It took a couple of times to relocate the track, but we did. We went on for about fifteen more minutes and Nasdaq found his reward.

A case of handler error, Nasdaq did perfectly. As they say in K9 Academy parlance, a case of "dope on a rope".

We did other tracks with the other teams, both of which were very long and time consuming. Before we knew it, the day was at an end.

We departed at 4:00 PM.

January 31, 2004 (Day 43) Weather clear & cold +17 Degrees

Tonight it was the Lt., Scott Paul from Fall River and me for training and we went to the Dever School in Taunton. We agreed to start the training an hour early because of the expected severe cold. We showed up at 6:00 PM (an hour early). We borrowed the keys to a small heated house and used that as our base of operation.

Officer Paul and I then took turns donning the bite suit and hiding in the boiler room down in the cellar. All the lights were turned out and our scent was allowed to build up for about twenty minutes. The dogs were sent (each during its own exercise and not together) without a muzzle and forced to find the bad guy using only its sense of smell, not its eyesight.

Both dogs did well. When it was Nasdaq's turn I gave the customary three warnings and then sent him down the stairs to find and bite the "bad guy". Took him about one minute to find him. When I heard the screams of "I give up" I rushed down shouting "don't fight the dog, let me see your hands!". He did as instructed and after being searched by the Lt. he was allowed to escape so that Nasdaq would stay "pumped".

After this we all went outside and Officer Paul again got into the bite suit and hid in the back of a large delivery truck that was open and parked in one of the parking lots. It was full of empty cardboard boxes.
